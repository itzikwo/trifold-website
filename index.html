The problem

The gap is rarely the technology

Executives know AI matters. Teams run pilots. Very little reaches production. After three decades in enterprise technology, most recently at the Data & AI team of IBM Israel, I watched this pattern repeat across pharma, financing, insurance, hospitality, and media.

The blocker is almost never the model. It is alignment between the business, the data and IT teams, and governance. Three folds: business, data and IT, governance. Line them up and AI ships. Leave one out and you get demos.

Why fractional

Seniority when you need it, not headcount you can’t justify yet

Most organizations are still early in AI adoption. Hiring a full-time Chief AI Officer before the business case exists tends to backfire: the role turns theoretical, expectations outrun execution, and the budget drifts.

The fractional model gives you the seniority when you need it, at a fraction of the cost, and hands more ownership to your own people as they grow into it.
